## Rejected registers -- do not produce these again

- Transition-forward openings: "Career changer...", "Transitioning from..." as the lead.
  Transition is implied, never announced.
- Corporate register: "actionable specifications", "drive initiatives through deployment",
  "remediate the underlying process", "principal analytical capability"
- Conversational first person: "I take a business question and get the data behind it"
- Clunky third-person: "built, owned, and acted on by the same person"
- Keyword-category recitation: "Business acumen: ..." as a labeled skills line
- Em dashes anywhere. They become colons or periods.
- JD-echo bullets: rewording his work into the posting's sentences. Tailoring is selection
  and emphasis, never rewording facts.
- Abstract bullets that strip out industry context. The reader must know this is real estate.
- Passive/vague constructions: "Responsible for", "Helped with", "Tasked with", "Involved in"
- AI as a skill or differentiator: no "AI-assisted analysis", no "AI coding agents",
  no "AI development pipeline" in the summary or subtitle. 65% of hiring managers say
  AI-enhanced resumes make it harder to assess skills. Mentioning AI as a skill signals
  "uses ChatGPT instead of knowing SQL." Remove all AI references from summary and subtitle.
- Summary that leads with background/origin: "Data analyst with ten years..." or
  "Experienced professional who..." The summary must lead with what Ben DOES for employers.
- Generic subtitle labels: "Data Analyst | Real Estate Analytics | AI-Assisted Analysis"
  Subtitle should be tools/capabilities: "SQL | Power BI | Dashboards & Business Reporting"
- Vague system references: "four integrated systems" without naming them. Name the systems:
  CRM, MLS, transaction management, bookkeeping.

## Bullet formula reference (Resume Worded 2026)

Pattern: Action verb + what you did + tool used + quantified result + business outcome

Example from Resume Worded:
"Analyzed sales data using SQL and Python to identify trends, resulting in a 10% boost in
revenue and a 20% reduction in customer churn"

Adapted for Ben:
"Analyzed lead conversion data in Excel and CRM reporting to identify which prospecting
metrics predicted closed real estate transactions, then rebuilt the team's KPI reporting
around those indicators to cut time spent on low-probability leads."

Key rules:
1. Tools woven INTO the bullet, not just listed in Skills
2. Industry (real estate) named explicitly
3. Quantify with numbers that survive an interview question
4. Strong action verb at the start (Analyzed, Built, Designed, Wrote, Reconciled, Managed)
5. No orphan skills: every skill in the Skills section also appears in a bullet
