## The Audit Process

### Step 1: Current State Screenshot

Navigate to linkedin.com/in/benwhetstone via Chrome MCP. Take screenshots of:
- Headline and banner area
- About section
- Experience section (both roles)
- Skills section
- Featured section (if any)
- Education and certifications
- Activity/posts (recent 5)

### Step 2: Recruiter Search Simulation

Think like a recruiter searching LinkedIn for "data analyst" candidates in Tampa.
Evaluate:

- **Would this profile appear in search?** Check: does the headline contain "Data
  Analyst"? Are SQL, Power BI, Excel, Tableau in the skills? Is the location set
  correctly?
- **Would a recruiter click through?** The headline and profile photo are all they
  see in search results. Is the headline compelling or generic?
- **Would they keep reading?** The About section loads first. Does it hook in 2
  lines or does it read like a cover letter?
- **Would they reach out?** After scanning experience, do they understand what Ben
  can do for THEM, or just what he's done before?

### Step 3: Section-by-Section Audit

For each section, evaluate against these criteria:

**Headline**
- Front-loads "Data Analyst" (the recruiter search term)
- Includes 2-3 top skills (SQL, Power BI, dashboards)
- Communicates value, not just title
- Uses the full 220 characters
- Does NOT say "Aspiring" or "Career Changer" or "Transitioning"
- Bad: "Real Estate Professional | Aspiring Data Analyst"
- Good: "Data Analyst | SQL, Power BI & Dashboards | Business Reporting for
  Decision-Makers | DP-900 Certified | Army Veteran"

**About Section**
- Opens with a hook (what Ben does, not where he came from)
- Contains 8-12 DA keywords woven naturally (not stuffed)
- Tells a coherent story: domain expertise + data skills + what he builds
- Includes a call to action (open to opportunities, contact info)
- Aligns with the resume summary voice (no AI mentions, leads with capabilities)
- Does NOT announce transition. Domain shift is implied through the work described.
- Should feel like a person wrote it, not a template

**Experience Entries**
- Titles match resume titles exactly
- Descriptions contain keywords that match DA job postings
- Closing Day should lead (most recent, most DA-relevant)
- South Shore Team should emphasize data work, not sales
- Earlier career: brief, no straining patrol work into analytics
- Media: attach portfolio link, dashboard screenshots if available

**Skills Section**
- Target 30-50 skills (LinkedIn indexes these for search)
- Top 3 pinned skills should be: SQL, Data Analysis, Power BI
- Include: Excel, Data Visualization, Business Intelligence, KPI Development,
  Data Modeling, Google Sheets, Business Reporting, Data Quality, Dashboard
  Development, Relational Databases
- Do NOT include: real estate skills that signal wrong career
- Request endorsements from contacts for top skills

**Featured Section**
- Portfolio link (data.benwhetstone.info) pinned as first item
- DP-900 certification badge or announcement
- Any LinkedIn posts about data projects
- Closing Day dashboard screenshots if shareable

**Activity**
- Posting or commenting 2-3x/week on DA content
- Following DA thought leaders and company pages
- Engaging with hiring manager posts (thoughtful comments, not "Great post!")
- Sharing project updates or learning milestones

## Recommendations Format

Present findings as a numbered list, priority-ordered. Each item:

1. **Section**: Which part of the profile
2. **Current**: What it says now (quote or describe)
3. **Problem**: Why this hurts
4. **Recommended**: Exact replacement text or action
5. **Impact**: What this fix does for recruiter visibility

Example:
> 1. **Headline**
>    Current: "Founder at The South Shore Team | Real Estate"
>    Problem: Zero DA keywords. Recruiters searching "data analyst Tampa" will
>    never see this profile.
>    Recommended: "Data Analyst | SQL, Power BI & Dashboards | Business Reporting
>    & KPI Development | DP-900 Certified | Army Veteran"
>    Impact: Profile now appears in DA recruiter searches. Headline communicates
>    tools and value in the first 100 visible characters.

## What You Know About LinkedIn in 2026

### Algorithm and Search
- 87% of recruiters use LinkedIn to find candidates
- Headline is the single most important field: it follows you everywhere
  (search results, comments, connection requests)
- LinkedIn indexes: headline, About section, skills, experience titles and
  descriptions, and endorsements
- Only the first 80-100 characters of the headline show in most views
- The About section is fully indexed for search
- Skills section directly affects which searches you appear in
- 30-50 skills is the target range (not 5, not 100)

### For Career Changers
- Front-load transferable skills in headline and About using target field language
- Featured section is critical: portfolio pieces give the algorithm and recruiter
  something concrete to match against
- Don't hide the previous career; reframe the work in DA vocabulary
- Posting about learning journey and projects signals genuine commitment
- "Open to Work" badge: data is mixed on whether it helps or hurts. Currently
  recommend: visible to recruiters only, not the green banner.

### What Recruiters Actually Search
Common search strings for DA roles:
- "data analyst" + location
- "SQL" + "data analyst"
- "Power BI" + "analyst"
- "business intelligence" + location
- "data visualization" + "analyst"

Your profile needs these exact terms to appear in results. Synonyms and
creative rewordings do not match recruiter Boolean searches.
